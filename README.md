Overview & Executive Summary
Centurylong bubbles—from 1929 margins to 2008 CDOs and 2026 AI monoculture—all rely on unproven abstractions. Today's AI and quantum hype collide with physical grid and silicon walls. True resilience requires bounding probabilistic AI within deterministic control envelopes, enforcing hardware-bound cryptographic provenance, and aligning software architectures strictly with thermodynamics and mathematical reality.

Why We Must Look Backward: The Cost of Ignoring Safety for Profit
When financial gain and rapid market deployment become the sole motivating drivers—while fundamental system safety is treated as an afterthought—history repeats itself with ruthless precision. Across the past century, every major systemic collapse shares an identical psychological and structural signature: the willful blindness of institutions chasing short-term leverage at the expense of long-term structural integrity.

The 1929 Parallel: Margin lenders and investment trusts ignored mounting structural debt because the market was "permanently plateaued," until forced liquidations wiped out a generation of wealth.

The 2008 Parallel: Wall Street institutions pushed toxic synthetic CDOs because transaction fees and short-term yields rewarded volume, brushing aside mathematical warnings until the entire global credit system froze.

The Present Danger: Today, rushing unmediated, probabilistic AI agents into production environments and financing mega-clusters through unsustainable debt structures mirrors these exact patterns. When financial motive overrides architectural safety, we trade systemic stability for speculative velocity.

Reviewing these historical failures is not an academic exercise; it is an urgent reminder that software can scale exponentially on paper, but physical infrastructure, thermodynamic limits, and mathematical laws cannot be lobbied or renegotiated. Ignoring safety guarantees in pursuit of unchecked scale transforms software monoculture into a ticking systemic fuse.
